The problem it solves

Years of conversations sit inside WeChat's encrypted local store on your Mac — unsearchable, unbacked-up, and gone if the phone or account goes. Screenshots are not an archive.

Who it is for

Anyone who wants their own WeChat history backed up and searchable: before changing phones, accounts, or just for their own records.

What you get

  • SKILL.md with the full export: locate and copy the local store, read the key from your own machine, decrypt the copy.
  • Per-conversation Markdown plus a CSV index across the whole archive, with media linked and an export log.
  • A verification pass comparing sampled conversations against the phone.
  • Ground rules that keep it safe: copy-first, the live database is never touched, keys never leave the machine.

Honest limitations

Mac only, for an account signed in on that Mac — it reads the local store, not WeChat's servers. Voice messages and mini-program content typically export as placeholders, and the log says so. WeChat updates occasionally move the data folders; the workflow survives, the paths may need re-locating.

FAQ

Is this my data or does it pull from WeChat's servers?

Yours, from your machine. The skill works entirely on the local store of the account signed in on your Mac — nothing is requested from any server.

Could this damage my chat history?

The workflow is copy-first: the live database is never opened or modified, everything happens on a copy, and the export is verified against the phone before it is called done.

What formats do I end up with?

One Markdown file per conversation, a CSV index over everything, media copied alongside where it exists locally, and an export log noting anything that did not survive.

What about the other people in my chats?

Their words stay private. The export is a personal archive — the skill's ground rules are explicit that it is not material to publish or forward.
