What problem does it solve?

Most prompt packs sell volume: hundreds of fill-in-the-blank lines, most of them interchangeable, none of them telling you what to hand over, what to expect back, or how to tell whether the output is any good. So every session starts from zero — you re-explain your audience, re-argue about standards, and accept whatever comes back because there is no stated bar for it to clear. The work that actually eats a creator's week isn't writing one post. It's picking the right topic, not shipping a wrong fact, turning one piece into four, answering comments without sounding like a brand, and knowing at week's end what actually worked.

How does it work?

The eight jobs, written as eight systems. Every system has the same six parts: a Role that also states what the role refuses to do; an Input contract, exactly what you provide before it starts; the copy-paste Prompt with the rules built in; a fixed Output format so results stay comparable week to week; Acceptance criteria you can actually verify; and a Worked example — a complete run on fictional data, so you can see what passing looks like. The eight: Topic Scout (score candidates on timeliness, audience fit, verifiability), Headline Forge (per-platform titles whose curiosity gap the content pays off), Fact Gate (every claim extracted, two-source checked, blocked if it fails), Script Smith (60-second vertical scripts: shot list, voiceover, subtitle rhythm), Voice Check Lite (seven template habits, minimal fixes), Repurpose Engine (one long piece into four platform forms), Reply Crafter (peer-voice replies that never invent your experience), Weekly Retro (median-first numbers, graded attribution, exactly three next actions). Every system is included in full in English and in Chinese.

Honest limits

These prompts enforce a workflow; they don't guarantee results. A fact gate only works if you actually supply sources, and a retro is only as honest as the numbers you feed it. Voice Check is the lite version — the full editing discipline is sold separately as Wuhiufan Human Tone Polish. The prompts assume you are willing to paste in your own material (drafts, numbers, comment threads); they will not invent your data, and they are written to refuse when the input contract isn't met.
