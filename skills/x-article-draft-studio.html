The problem it solves

Pasting Markdown into the X Article composer loses things quietly: covers cropped to the wrong ratio, tables flattened into unreadable text, images landing in the wrong paragraph or vanishing past a media limit — and you find out after publishing.

Who it is for

Writers who draft long-form in Markdown and publish on X Articles, and who want the draft checked against the source before it goes anywhere near the publish button.

What you get

  • SKILL.md with the preflight: 5:2 cover preparation, an inline-image count, and wide-table handling that never mangles your source file.
  • The upload procedure in a separate browser session, building native table blocks at their positions instead of pasting them flat.
  • A reload-and-verify pass: title, image count and order, every table's rows and cells checked against the source.
  • A drafts-only rule and an overwrite guard: an existing draft is only touched when you name it and confirm in the same session.

Honest limitations

Drafts only, by design — publishing stays your click after reading the draft on X. X changes its composer without notice, so the procedure checks current limits in the composer rather than trusting fixed numbers. It needs your signed-in X session; you sign in yourself.

FAQ

Will it publish the article for me?

No. It stops at a verified draft, every time. Publishing is a human click after you read the draft on X — that boundary is the point of the workflow.

What happens to my tables?

They are built as native X table blocks at the right positions, then verified cell by cell after a reload. A table that arrives as plain text counts as a failure and gets rebuilt, not shipped.

My article has a lot of images. Is that a problem?

The preflight counts them first. If the count risks composer limits, the skill merges consecutive screenshots or splits the article — and never silently drops an image to make things fit.

Does it need my X password?

Never. It works with a session you signed into yourself, in a separate browser profile so your open windows are not hijacked.
