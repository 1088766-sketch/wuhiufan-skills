What problem does it solve?

Pet studios are expensive and most pets hate the trip. Asking an image model for nine portraits naively gives you nine different animals in the same sit-and-stare pose. This skill fixes that in three steps: it locks your pet's identity in writing first, plans the variety in advance, and lets you approve the whole set as one cheap preview grid before the real renders.

How does it work?

1. Drop in one clear, front-facing photo of your pet (side and back views help if you have them). 2. The skill builds an identity sheet — coat, markings, eyes, ears, build — and enforces it in every image. 3. You get a nine-shot plan mixing close-ups, mid shots and full-body frames, with a hard rule that no pose repeats more than twice. 4. You approve the 3x3 contact sheet and swap poses or props. 5. You receive nine finished portraits, generated individually from your original photo and quality-checked one by one, in portrait 3:4.

How do I install it?

Download the ZIP, unzip it into your agent's skills directory (for Claude Code: ~/.claude/skills/), restart the agent, and ask for the output in plain language. The included install guide covers Claude Code, Codex and Cursor step by step. You also need an image generation or editing tool that accepts reference photos — the skill is model-agnostic.

Frequently asked questions

What is the current status of this skill?

On sale now on Gumroad for $4.99. Example portraits shown for this skill are generated with the skill itself — we do not use third-party sample images.

Does it work for pets other than cats and dogs?

Yes. Rabbits, birds and small pets are covered, with poses and props adapted per species. Nothing is ever tied to or forced onto the animal.

Can I use my own theme instead of the included ones?

Yes. The theme packs are starting points; the shot-planning and identity systems work with any theme you describe.

What are the honest limits?

Results depend on your photo and your image model. A sharp front photo gives strong likeness; angles your photos never showed are the model's best guess. The contact sheet is a planning sketch, so tiny details can differ in the final individual renders.
